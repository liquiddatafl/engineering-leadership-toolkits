About Becky

Engineering leadership shaped by real-world experience.

I’m Becky Simmons, a seasoned technology leader who helps teams bring more structure, clarity, and consistency to complex technology environments. My work has focused on scaling engineering organizations, improving delivery practices, strengthening platform governance, and helping leaders make better decisions across enterprise technology and Salesforce ecosystems. Engineering Leadership Toolkits grew out of that work: practical resources designed to help leaders assess what’s working, identify where risk or friction is hiding, and move forward with a clearer path.

My background

My career has focused on software development, enterprise architecture, commerce technology, and Salesforce ecosystems. I currently work as a Director of Engineering, where I’m responsible for helping teams solve complex technical problems while balancing delivery, platform health, business priorities, and long-term sustainability.

Over the course of my career, I’ve worked across both hands-on technical disciplines and leadership. That perspective has shaped how I think about topics like technical debt, prioritization, architecture, team structure, engineering governance, and decision-making.

I also hold an MBA and have earned multiple Salesforce certifications across administration, development, architecture, commerce, and AI. I’m especially interested in the intersection of strong engineering practices and practical business decision-making.

Additionally, I've had the opportunity to speak at multiple Salesforce conferences, including Dreamforce, sharing practical lessons from enterprise engineering, AI, architecture, commerce, and technology leadership.

The resources here are built from that perspective. They are not intended to add ceremony for its own sake. They are designed to help engineering managers and technical leads create enough structure to make difficult conversations clearer and decisions easier to act on.

How I approach engineering leadership

Clarity over complexity Good frameworks should make decisions easier, not create another layer of bureaucracy.
Business and engineering belong together Technical decisions are stronger when teams understand both the engineering impact and the business context.
Not everything needs to be fixed Good engineering leadership is often about making deliberate tradeoffs, not trying to eliminate every imperfection.
